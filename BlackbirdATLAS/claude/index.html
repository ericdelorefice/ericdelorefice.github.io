How To: Exercise Form

Hand Release Push Up

  • Chest, hips, and thighs go down together
  • Lift both hands off the ground
  • Press to full lockout
  • Body stays in one line

Sit Up

  • Shoulder blades touch the ground
  • Elbows reach the knees
  • Feet held by a partner
  • Controlled tempo

2-Mile Run Pacing

  • Run the first lap at goal split, not faster
  • Know your 400 m split
  • Relax shoulders and hands
  • Finish the last 400 m strong

Plank

  • Forearms on the ground
  • Straight line from head to heels
  • Squeeze glutes and brace the core
  • Breathe

Reference: Nutrition, Bloodwork, and Recovery

Macros

If you train 5 days a week, your food has to keep up. Protein helps your muscles repair after lifting and running. Carbs are your main fuel for runs and hard sessions. Fats support your hormones and give you steady energy. A simple approach is to build each meal around a protein source, a carb, and some healthy fat. This is general guidance, not a personal nutrition plan.

Understanding Your Bloodwork

A provider may check these common markers:

  • Vitamin D: helps keep bones strong, which matters when you run a lot of miles.
  • Iron / ferritin: iron helps carry oxygen in your blood, and low levels can make runs feel much harder.
  • B12: helps your body make red blood cells and turn food into energy.

Talk to a medical provider before taking any supplement.
